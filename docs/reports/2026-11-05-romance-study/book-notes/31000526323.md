The entire eight-file free window covers roughly one day: Jessica is abandoned at the courthouse, overhears Xavier's missing hired bride problem, offers herself, marries him, learns his wealth, meets his dying grandfather and is introduced to his household. The two needs are established separately before converging. Her immediate motive is avoiding the humiliation of going home unmarried; his is meeting a grandfather's marriage condition for inheritance and alleviating the older man's fear of his isolation. Their shared bereavement offers a potential emotional bridge beneath the transactional situation.

Investment initially rests on Jessica's pleading and discovery that Burke is with her friend Emma. Her spontaneous proposal is the decisive plot action, though the bargain is poorly informed: Xavier privately assumes a one-year divorce and tells her to fake a PhD, while she is still learning his name, profession and family. She does not become emotionally healed just because the ring is expensive; she still longs for Burke. Warmth first grows through Dawson's affection and Jessica's volunteered visit/care, not sexual attraction or a polished contract negotiation. Xavier's economical speech, her questions and the household's disbelief provide mild comedy.

Contract Marriage and Heir/Heirness supply the setup; Betrayal supplies her trigger; CEO appears as status revelation; Independent has limited realization beyond proposing and her existing assistant-chef job. Comedy and drama coexist, with no revenge payoff yet. The synopsis accurately advertises the coincidence and future co-residence romance, while the body spends much of the window establishing names, appearances, family concern and home transition. It does not demonstrate eventual love, grandfather death or divorce terms accepted by both. This differs from the strategic equal-partner contract of A Woman Worth Billions: the same tag can mean an impulsive, information-asymmetric marriage of emotional necessity.
