The opening turns a job search into a marriage contract, then skips five months to show how performed intimacy has become emotionally real for Amelia but disposable for Ryan. His old crush's return makes him terminate the arrangement early and condition payment on her leaving New York. She discovers pregnancy after moving to Texas, chooses to raise the child, and five years later has built a financial-analysis career. Dora's question about her father motivates Amelia to cautiously admit James, a colleague, into their lives. Several chapters invest in his courtesy, meals, childcare awareness and patient intimacy before Ryan arrives with Dora, having collected her from school. The interruption threatens an already functioning alternative family, not merely a lonely heroine awaiting her original partner.

Emotional investment begins in material precarity (electricity cut off, job inexperience) and becomes betrayal, maternal responsibility, then renewed domestic pleasure. Amelia signs under financial pressure, later chooses secrecy and a new relationship; the costs include isolation, concealed paternity and repeated lies to Dora. James's uninvited tracking of her home is narrated as a forgivable romantic surprise, a useful contradiction to the otherwise consent-aware kiss scene. The ending of the free window puts the child's enthusiasm against Amelia's alarm.
