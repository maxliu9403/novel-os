# A Ruling Passion: Mr Tremont's Priceless Little Bride

Book ID: 21000003253; assignment 3.

Read coverage: official synopsis and all 21 supplied free chapter files, complete contents. This does not mean the complete published novel was read.

Supplied genre: ['Romance']; tags: Campus, Independent, Heir/Heirness, Age Gap, Misunderstanding, Drama, Dark Romance, Hidden Identity.

Across 21 complete free chapters the principal engine is coercive dependence punctuated by ambiguous care, not an already mutual romance. Orphaned Arianne has lived from age eight under Mark, ten years older, who blames her pilot father for the crash that killed his parents. At the opening she is nearly eighteen, impoverished within a wealthy home, working and studying art while resisting dependence. Mark's return interrupts friendships and a potential relationship with Will: he sends Will abroad, anonymously funds her studies, controls food, transport and gifts, and demands exclusive recourse to his money. The narration supplies his concealed concern alongside her fear, creating dramatic irony but never cancelling the coercion shown.

Her eighteenth birthday, subsequent illness and a knife attack each shift their proximity. Sexual pressure is followed by gifts; she interprets money as payment and refuses to spend it. A tutor's cruelty produces a hospital rescue, where Tiffany unknowingly tells Mark how damaging his guardianship has been. Arianne's clearest assertion is a plan to finish studies, repay him and move out; he responds with confinement and a sexual assault attempt that he stops only when she becomes emotionally vacant. Later she shields him from a knife, resulting in intimate nursing and further restrictions on school. The free window ends when she attends Will's return party while believing Mark is away, drinks, wakes in changed clothes beside Will without memory, and a public photo brings Mark back in rage. The photo is a provocation; these chapters do not establish what happened overnight.

Emotional investment resides in deprivation, artistic talent, Tiffany's friendship, small comforts and the hope of an alternative life. Arianne is not devoid of choices: she works, conceals gifts, rejects money, argues for independence and chooses the party. Each choice has credible costs inside the story's coercive system. Mary repeatedly interprets food and handsomeness as reasons to accept Mark, while Tiffany criticizes the unknown guardian yet idealizes Mark publicly; the gap between social reputation and private experience is structurally important. Care is personalized (tea, shoes, food, bedside presence), but often made inseparable from control.

Campus is an active institution (sponsorship, authority, tutor, public event), Age Gap includes guardian dependency, Independent is an aspiration under pressure, and Dark Romance accurately signals the violent possessive dynamic. Misunderstanding is partly asymmetrical information about care, but some harms are explicit acts, not misunderstandings. Hidden Identity concerns her undisclosed connection to a publicly admired man, not a demonstrated secret royal/CEO revelation. The official synopsis makes the crash/debt/hatred frame very clear; it omits the extended friendship, bodily hardship, institutional power and free-window party cliffhanger. No fixed Romance template can safely replace these particular dependencies or equate his attention with consent or accomplished redemption.
