# Genre Adapters

Use this reference after the core story contract is clear. Keep the same five-force engine across genres, then change the evidence, pressure, and world constraints that make decisions difficult.

## Commercial execution styles

When `commercial_story` is active, express these studied styles through the
shared contract fields rather than adding new schema keys:

- Domestic ethics: family labor, permission, money, time, and space.
- Motherhood: child safety, body, future, and age-credible child behavior.
- Professional erasure: name, labor, version history, records, and work process.
- Departure and revenge: seeded leverage, withdrawn support, and fair effects.
- Illness and grief: care, time, bodily choice, memory, and shared history.
- Romantic repair: reliable action, shared tasks, boundaries, and tested trust.
- Suspense and crime: narrowing evidence, deadlines, knowledge, and institutions.
- Speculative fantasy: rule-bound power, social cost, identity, and belonging.

Choose the controlled premise categories from `commercial-story-design.md`.
Genre conventions may shape scenes and voice, but do not change the contract
identity or import a sample plot chain.

## Realistic contemporary and emotional fiction

- External objectives: secure housing, protect a child, recover income, finish a case, repair a reputation, or leave a coercive institution.
- Adaptive pressure: money, schedules, childcare, workplace policy, family expectations, health, law, and public narrative.
- Ledgers: cash flow, contracts, medical or school logistics, legal process, evidence provenance, and relationship boundaries.
- Quality test: hardship changes choices and resources; it does not appear only as decorative suffering.

## Mystery, crime, and suspense

- External objectives: identify a perpetrator, prevent a deadline event, prove a hidden history, or protect a witness.
- Adaptive pressure: a suspect changes tactics, evidence degrades, access closes, and time or jurisdiction narrows.
- Ledgers: scene chronology, access and opportunity, evidence source, chain of custody, character knowledge, false explanations, and payoff windows.
- Quality test: every major reversal has at least two earlier facts, a plausible motive, and a changed next action.

## Fantasy and speculative fiction

- External objectives: cross a threshold, protect a faction, break a binding, deliver a resource, or decide who controls a dangerous capability.
- Adaptive pressure: rules respond predictably, factions learn, costs accumulate, and exceptions carry social consequences.
- Ledgers: rule trigger, observable effect, limit, price, exception evidence, people who know, resource inventory, faction alignment, and geography.
- Quality test: powers solve one problem while creating a visible cost; exceptions are foreshadowed before they matter.

## Science fiction

- External objectives: keep a habitat alive, expose a governance failure, complete a mission, or choose who receives scarce capacity.
- Adaptive pressure: energy, latency, system permissions, maintenance, institutional incentives, and irreversible technical consequences.
- Ledgers: capability boundary, data provenance, authority, failure mode, time delay, resource burn, social impact, and competing objectives.
- Quality test: technology changes what characters can choose and what those choices cost; it never acts as unexplained magic.

## Historical or period fiction

- External objectives: survive a political transition, protect a household, win patronage, preserve evidence, or change a local power arrangement.
- Adaptive pressure: status, law, custom, travel speed, communication limits, family obligations, and institutional violence.
- Ledgers: date, place, social position, available knowledge, material culture, travel duration, legal custom, and who can safely speak.
- Quality test: characters act within period constraints while retaining individual motives; modern attitudes appear only with a credible story reason.

## Workplace, campus, and professional fiction

- External objectives: deliver a project, secure a role, expose misconduct, pass a gate, or build an independent livelihood.
- Adaptive pressure: hierarchy, budgets, deadlines, evaluation rules, reputation, team incentives, and scarce opportunities.
- Ledgers: decision rights, evidence, deliverables, resource owners, deadlines, stakeholder goals, and consequences of failure.
- Quality test: professional wins show the work and tradeoff behind the result, not a single speech or public humiliation.

## Mixed genres

Choose one primary engine and one supporting lens. State which rules win when they conflict. For example, a romantic mystery keeps relationship choices central while evidence rules govern what can be known; a fantasy family drama keeps family boundaries central while magic rules govern the cost of intervention.
