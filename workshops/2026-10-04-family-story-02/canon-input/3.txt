
"Evelyn, about Olivia’s special education sessions for next quarter... can we scale them back to once every two weeks?"
At night, Christian sat at the dining table, flipping through the family expense statements with his eyebrows knit tight.
"Her therapy fees are getting ridiculous at this stage," he grumbled, not looking up. "We need to be smart about our household budget."
I stopped wiping down the kitchen counter.
In the past, I would have frantically pulled up research papers to explain how crucial continuous sensory integration was for her dyslexia.
Tonight, I just said coldly, "Her therapist said Olivia is finally making progress."
"Don't be so dramatic all the time." Christian snapped, his impatience flaring up.
"My firm is pitching for the master landscape contract over at Spruce Valley. Once I land that deal, we’ll have all the cash for top-tier tutors."
"Right now, Olivia needs to learn to self-study like normal kids."
I stayed silent, a cold, bitter irony washing over me.
"Spruce Valley" was the most exclusive luxury residential development in the region. Christian genuinely believed his tiny, struggling firm had impressed their board of directors on its own merit.
What he didn't know was that the Chief Executive Director of the entire Spruce Valley Project was Jack—my former senior fellow from the Linnaeus Institute.
Three days ago, Jack had called me personally: "Evelyn, out of respect for your professional reputation and your recommendation, I’m giving your husband a spot in the private bidding. Don't let him disappoint me."
I had quietly used my own connections, my prestige, and my standing in the industry to secretly keep his business afloat.
And in return, he was calculating how to cut our daughter’s therapy funds.
At that exact moment, it hit me with terrifying clarity: In a world driven purely by leverage, a stay-at-home mother who had been out of the workforce for two years had zero power—she had to beg her husband just to secure basic care for her child.
The final blow came the very next morning.
I went into Christian’s home office to organize his presentation files. As I picked up a stack of paper near the printer, a loose receipt slipped out and fell onto the floor.
It was a quarterly statement from the Pegasus Royal Equestrian Club.
Cardholder: Carter Hayes (Helena’s son)
Amount Billed: $12,000
Payer: Christian Davis
Stapled to the back of the bill was a handwritten note from Helena in elegant cursive:
"Christian, thank you for your incredible generosity. You are more of a father to Carter than anyone else ever was. I only regret that I didn't say yes to you all those years ago."
Twelve thousand dollars.
That single charge could have paid for a full year of Olivia’s special education sessions.
The man who had slammed the door on his daughter’s therapy budget had effortlessly dropped twelve grand on his ex-girlfriend’s son.
All to buy himself a cheap, ego-stroking compliment.
My knuckles turned white as I crushed the receipt in my hand.
I didn't march out to confront him. I didn't scream.
I refused to waste another drop of emotion on anyone other than my daughter.
Instead, I took out my phone, snapped crystal-clear photos of the receipt, the handwritten note, and the bank transfers showing Christian secretly funneling money from our joint business account.
Then, I opened the professional email address I hadn't logged into for two long years.
Waiting in my inbox were several unread recruitment inquiries from major headhunters offering Landscape Director positions.
My fingers flew across the keyboard as I replied to the top firm:
"Hello, this is Evelyn. Regarding the Landscape Director role we discussed previously—I am ready for the interview."
